# Non-biblical Books — Spec Addendum

Adds a general **non-biblical book** capability to Scripture Study. *Humility* by Andrew Murray (1895) is its first concrete instance and *IN* (A Team, 2026) its second; both are paragraph Books. *1 Enoch* — R. H. Charles's 1912 English, Edition Code `1EN-C1912` — is the third and the first verse-atom Book. Full integration parity: a book is readable, referencable, and cross-referencable. This document consolidates every decision from the wayfinder maps [#50](https://github.com/chfourie/obsidian-bible-study/issues/50) and [#112](https://github.com/chfourie/obsidian-bible-study/issues/112); it extends the [v1 specification](spec.md) and follows the glossary in [CONTEXT.md](../CONTEXT.md). Identity decisions: [ADR 0002](adr/0002-book-atoms-extend-bcv-id-space.md), [ADR 0005](adr/0005-book-atom-is-printed-citable-unit.md). Source-text research: [humility-source-text.md](research/humility-source-text.md), [charles-1912-apparatus-and-source.md](research/charles-1912-apparatus-and-source.md), [charles-1912-volume-anatomy.md](research/charles-1912-volume-anatomy.md). Visual reference: [prototypes/book-reader-prototype](../prototypes/book-reader-prototype).

Books ride the existing machinery everywhere — no parallel book paths. Where a decision below names a particular Book, the general rule is stated first and that Book is its instance.

---

## 1. Identity & addressing

([ADR 0002](adr/0002-book-atoms-extend-bcv-id-space.md), [ADR 0005](adr/0005-book-atom-is-printed-citable-unit.md))

- Books extend the `BBBCCCVVV` id space: scripture keeps 1–66, 67–100 reserved for canon extensions, non-biblical books start at **101** (Humility = 101). Numbers are assigned by the repo-side **Book Registry** (§7), append-only, never reused.
- **Atom = the work's smallest printed citable unit** ([ADR 0005](adr/0005-book-atom-is-printed-citable-unit.md)): a paragraph where the print has paragraphs, a printed verse where it has verses. `CCC` = section, `VVV` = atom within section. *Humility* and *IN* are paragraph Books, so for them `VVV` is the paragraph, as it always was. Epigraphs are chapter metadata, not atoms; Footnotes attach to their anchor atom.
- **Atom kind is a property of the Book**, not of a section or an atom: `verse` | `paragraph`, one kind for the whole Book, so front matter is never a mixed `par.` / `v.` family. It is declared as `atom` on the Book's **Book Registry** entry (§7) and copied into the manifest's `book` sub-object; the field is optional and defaults to `paragraph`, so *Humility* and *IN* carry none and load exactly as today. `BookSection.paragraphs` keeps its name as the atom count — renaming it would force the format-version bump [ADR 0009](adr/0009-optional-book-channels-do-not-bump-format-version.md) rules out for an optional field an older Book loads correctly without.
- **Sections** take chapter numbers in reading order, keeping printed chapter numbers. Humility: Preface = 0, chapters I–XII = 1–12, Notes A–D = 13–16, A Prayer for Humility = 17 (18 sections; the source's Note D was missed by the planning research and surfaced at build time — see ADR 0002).
- The manifest's section table is the book's versification data. The **first published module release freezes the grid** — later releases may fix text within atoms but never renumber. One book = one module = one grid; editions are not translations.
- A single manifest **edition code** (Humility: `HUM-M1895`; 1 Enoch: `1EN-C1912`) fills the translation slot in all keying, and the reader shows it as the single non-switchable edition pill — `Humility 1895`, `1 Enoch 1912` — never a translation switcher (§5). Fallback Translation never applies to books: a passage the installed module cannot answer is the explicit module-absent error, never a substituted English (§6).
- **Identity is registry-locked before the Book's first build** (§7), so it is not re-litigated at build time. *1 Enoch*: book **103**, title `1 Enoch`, abbreviation `1En`, aliases *First Enoch* / *Book of Enoch* / *Ethiopic Enoch*, author **Enoch** — the ascription the work carries, not the editor of the edition — year **1912**, Edition Code `1EN-C1912`, module id `1en-c1912`, atom `verse`, US public domain (1912 publication; Charles d. 1931).
- **The citable grid is the translation, not the volume it was printed in** ([volume anatomy](research/charles-1912-volume-anatomy.md)). *1 Enoch*'s grid is Charles's English **1:1–108:N** — chapters I–CVIII as he numbered them, the Book of Noah (106–107) and the Appendix chapter (108) included — and whole-book `{1 Enoch}` is exactly that range (§3). **Charles-the-book is out of the first module**: the Preface, the General Introduction, the abbreviations-and-brackets key, the Special Introductions, Appendix I (the Gizeh and Syncellus Greek), Appendix II and the Indexes are no part of it, so the module is the translation plus its Footnotes rather than the 1912 volume-as-object. Appendix I's Greek is therefore never a `{1 Enoch …}` address — a Greek fragment is another text, not another verse of this grid. No "readable, non-citable section" kind is invented to carry any of it: a section is on the grid or it is not in the module.
- **A verse-atom section may carry no name.** *1 Enoch* is one section per printed chapter, 1–108. Charles titled few of them, so a verse-atom section head may be `## 5.` with no name; the section table's `name` is then the printed chapter number as a string (`"5"`), and the tree row, the breadcrumb option and the Title Bar show that number alone — Title Bar *1 Enoch* 5, matching the chip locator (§4) — rather than growing an invented name. A verse-atom chapter the print does title keeps `## N. name`. Paragraph Books are unchanged: a name is required, and display-named sections keep `named: true`.
- **Parts are 1 Enoch's constituent books**, listed in the tree as non-selectable group labels off the section table's `part` (§5): Watchers 1–36, Parables 37–71, Heavenly Luminaries 72–82, Dream-Visions 83–90, Epistle 91–105, Book of Noah 106–107, Appendix 108. Charles's finer heads are `###` **Headings** attached to the verse they precede — furniture beside the grid, never atoms.
- The plugin discovers which books exist from installed module manifests; scripture's 66 books stay compiled-in.

## 2. Source text & build pipeline

([humility-source-text.md](research/humility-source-text.md), [charles-1912-apparatus-and-source.md](research/charles-1912-apparatus-and-source.md))

- **The Book path is the shared markdown book pipeline**: curated Markdown source → `scripts/book-pipeline/` (shared parser, Book Registry validation, artifact build), driven by a thin per-Book build script (`scripts/build-in-module.mjs`). *IN* shipped through it and every Book from here takes the same path — a new Book adds curated source plus its build script, never a parser of its own. *1 Enoch* is the next Book down it: curated Markdown from the source below plus a thin build script of its own, the same registry check and artifact shape as *IN*.
- **What the shared pipeline does for a Book** is selected by the Book's atom kind (§1, §7): the paragraph parser for a paragraph Book, the `N.` / `Na.` verse-line parser for a verse-atom Book (§11). On either it strips Editorial-mark wrappers into `supplied` / `marks` / `emended` spans (§10), lifts `[Footnote…]` markers onto the `footnotes` channel (§6), scans Ref Spans (§8), and emits `reading` only where a section's page order is not the identity walk (§11). Every offset it writes indexes the final stored string (§6).
- **Humility's Gutenberg parser is history**: `scripts/build-humility-module.mjs` + vitest-covered `humility-pipeline/` read Project Gutenberg plain text directly — chapter heads (`^(I..XII)\.$` + title line) and blank-line paragraphs yield section.paragraph atoms, epigraphs parsed from their fixed `_'…'_--REV. iv. 11` shape. It remains the builder of the one module it built; it is not the path a Book takes now, and *1 Enoch* does not take it.
- **A public-domain Book's source is two things**: a hand-proofed digitization as the **build source**, and page images of the edition as the **verification authority** — never a scan's own OCR, never a digitization known to be incomplete. The digitizer's boilerplate is stripped, its name included wherever its license would otherwise forbid textual fixes; what remains is unrestricted and ships as a GitHub release artifact (§7).
- **Source (Humility): Project Gutenberg eBook #57121** (plain text UTF-8, Fleming H. Revell edition, hand-keyed). Public domain worldwide (1895 work, Murray d. 1917). Strip everything outside PG's `*** START/END ***` markers **including the PG name** (keeping it would forbid textual fixes); the remaining text is unrestricted and safe to ship as a GitHub release artifact.
- **Verification authority (Humility)**: the Internet Archive scan [`humilitybeautyof00murr`](https://archive.org/details/humilitybeautyof00murr) (1910 Revell printing) — page-image authority for build-time fixes, never a build source. Three known transcription blemishes are fixed at build time against it (listed in the research doc).
- **Humility build**: mirroring the BSB build, Gutenberg #57121 → `dist/hum-m1895-module/`.
- **Source (1 Enoch): Project Gutenberg eBook #77935** — the SPCK 1917 reprint of Charles's 1912 Clarendon translation, plain-text UTF-8, PGDP hand-proofed from page images, chapters I–CVIII all present with the marks largely intact (ticket 113, [research](research/charles-1912-apparatus-and-source.md)). Strip everything outside PG's markers **including the PG name**, the same license pattern as *Humility*; the remaining English is **US public domain** (1912 publication; Charles d. 1931) and ships as a GitHub release artifact (§7) in a later implementation effort, not this spec's. Anything the reprint prints that is not Charles's English 1:1–108:N is outside the grid (§1) and therefore not in the module.
- **Verification authority (1 Enoch)**: the 1912 Clarendon page images on the Internet Archive — Cornell [`cu31924067146773`](https://archive.org/details/cu31924067146773) or Toronto Robarts [`bookofenochor1en00char`](https://archive.org/details/bookofenochor1en00char) — for every disputed reading, bracket *type* and verse break, so glyphs and lineation follow 1912 rather than a digitization's choices. The stored glyphs are the 1912 print's, written per §10; how a digitization's glyph and emphasis conventions map onto them is a curation detail for the build effort, not a rule here. **Rejected as build sources**: Wikisource (incomplete front matter; a phantom chapter 109 — §3), sacred-texts (damaged Editorial marks: 1:4's bracket type, 108:4's lost daggers), and the IA scans' own OCR (bracket glyphs destroyed, notes interleaved with the translation).
- **Footnote source (1 Enoch)**: the first module ships a **curated subset** of Charles's 1912 notes, not the full apparatus. A Footnote (§6) exists where a verse carries a `marks` or `emended` span (§10) and Charles's note explains that mark — an emendation, a dagger, a lacuna, a restoration, a bracket. Supplied words are not marks and earn no note. PG #77935 carries none of the notes, so their text is **hand-transcribed from the 1912 Clarendon page images** — the verification authority above — into the curated Markdown's `[Footnote…]` markers (§6): never from the scans' OCR, never from APOT (1913), whose condensed wording is another edition's under a `1EN-C1912` Edition Code. The build **warns, never fails**, on a marked atom with no Footnote; the warning list is the curation to-do, not a gate, and is driven to zero by a note or by a **conscious waiver** — a curator comment beside the atom (`%% waived C:V — reason`) where the page gives the mark no note of its own; a waiver naming an atom that is not on the to-do fails the build (ticket 146). Exegetical notes and variant-reading notes on unmarked verses are a later content-only release ([ADR 0009](adr/0009-optional-book-channels-do-not-bump-format-version.md): no format bump). Rejected: shipping no notes (the paint would show marks with no "why"); the full apparatus (hand-curating 272 pages before the grid can ship).

## 3. Reference grammar

([ADR 0005](adr/0005-book-atom-is-printed-citable-unit.md), [ADR 0010](adr/0010-line-letters-are-locators-reading-order-may-interleave-atoms.md))

- **Typing is numeric only**, exactly the scripture grammar over the Book's grid, and the grammar is the same whatever the Book's atom kind (§1) — a verse-atom Book adds no form and loses none (ticket 117). Every scripture form is available: verse `{Humility 5:2}` / `{1 Enoch 1:9}`, range `{Humility 5:2-4}` / `{1 Enoch 1:9-11}`, comma list `{1 Enoch 1:9,11}`, whole chapter `{Humility 5}` / `{1 Enoch 1}`, chapter range `{Humility 1-2}` / `{1 Enoch 1-2}`, cross-chapter range `{1 Enoch 1:9-2:3}`, whole book `{Humility}` / `{1 Enoch}`. `{1 Enoch 1:9}` is Charles chapter 1, verse 9. Special sections are typed by chapter number (`{Humility 0:3}` = Preface, `{Humility 14:1}` = Note B); autocomplete surfaces the section-name ↔ number mapping. Name-based section sugar is a possible later additive.
- **Name resolution** from installed module manifests only: canonical name (`Humility`), short abbreviation (`Hum`), optional aliases, matched case-insensitively via the existing `matchBook` path (3-word max stays; longer titles rely on an alias). Scripture's compiled table always wins collisions; manifest names shadowing scripture are dropped at load with a console warning. *1 Enoch* resolves from the canonical name `1 Enoch`, the abbreviation `1En`, and the aliases *First Enoch*, *Book of Enoch* and *Ethiopic Enoch*, so any of the ways the work is actually named opens the same Book. Cross-book uniqueness is enforced repo-side by the Book Registry at registration time. An uninstalled book's name is unknown → the whole `{...}` renders as plain text (existing safety valve).
- **Option tokens**: translation ids are never valid on a book reference (highlighted-and-ignored, like any invalid token); the edition code is not accepted as a token either. Display keywords and highlight cues work identically — highlights bind under the edition code in the translation slot.
- **Errors** mirror scripture: unknown book (incl. uninstalled module) or an address outside the module's section table → whole `{...}` plain text. The section table is the whole citable grid and nothing else is addressable. *1 Enoch*: the grid is the 1:1–108:N range §1 fixes, so `{1 Enoch}` means that range — while `{1 Enoch 0}` (Charles-the-book's own front matter, never on the grid) and `{1 Enoch 109}` (a phantom chapter of a digitization the build rejects) are both invalid and render as plain text.
- **A Line letter is never a cite** ([ADR 0010](adr/0010-line-letters-are-locators-reading-order-may-interleave-atoms.md)): letters are print locators on metrical lines, not atoms and not stored text, so `{1 Enoch 5:6a}` is not an address — it renders as plain text. The citable address is the printed verse, `{1 Enoch 5:6}`.

## 4. Rendering in notes

- All display modes apply — bare chip / `inline` / `block`. No separate callout type: the v1 "general non-bible reference block type" deferral is **superseded**.
- **Chip**: scripture's chip anatomy with a distinguishing mark (book icon / modifier class), same accent family, the Book's title in italics. The **locator family follows the Book's atom kind** (§1), and nothing else about the chip changes.
  - **Paragraph Books keep compact MLA-style locators**, exactly as today: *Humility* ch. 5, par. 2. Display-named sections replace the chapter locator: *Humility* Preface, par. 3 — the manifest section table flags them (`named: true`), set by the build for every section the printed work carries no chapter number for (Preface, Notes A–D, the Prayer).
  - **Verse-atom Books use scripture's numeric family**: *1 Enoch* 1:9 — the colon form scripture prints, carried by the italic title and book icon rather than a translation label. No `ch.` / `par.` on a verse-atom Book.
- **Verse-atom locators**, one per grammar form (§3):

  | Reference | Locator |
  | --- | --- |
  | `{1 Enoch 1:9}` | 1:9 |
  | `{1 Enoch 1:9-11}` | 1:9-11 |
  | `{1 Enoch 1:9,11}` | 1:9,11 |
  | `{1 Enoch 1}` | 1 |
  | `{1 Enoch 1-2}` | 1-2 |
  | `{1 Enoch 1:9-2:3}` | 1:9-2:3 |
  | `{1 Enoch}` | *(none)* |

- **One locator string per reference**, shared by the chip and the full citation, so a reader never meets two wordings for one address: the chip is *title* + locator, the full citation is `author, title (year), locator` — `Enoch, 1 Enoch (1912), 1:9`. The author slot is whatever the manifest carries (§7); for *1 Enoch* that is the Book Registry's Enoch ascription, not the editor of the edition — ticket 117's `R. H. Charles, 1 Enoch (1912), 1:9` illustrated the locator shape, not the ascription. A **whole-book** reference has no locator at all: `{1 Enoch}` cites `Enoch, 1 Enoch (1912)`, inventing no verse span.
- **`inline`** mirrors scripture whatever the atom kind: atom numbers superscripted only for multi-atom refs, so `{Humility 5:2}` and `{1 Enoch 1:9}` print clean text while `{1 Enoch 1:9-11}` prints ⁹ ¹⁰ ¹¹. Line letters never print inline ([ADR 0010](adr/0010-line-letters-are-locators-reading-order-may-interleave-atoms.md)) — a compact quote stays compact. Same 180-atom display cap as scripture.
- **`block`** renders normal prose paragraphs with superscript atom numbers (no one-atom-per-line) plus a full-citation attribution line: `Andrew Murray, Humility (1895), ch. 5, par. 2` (manifest supplies author, title, year). The same full citation renders wherever a one-time citation belongs (Study Panel header). A table atom prints as the same grid the reader prints (§5) — and is the one stretch of a passage that is not a highlight surface: its cells are text runs of their own, so a drag across them cannot be mapped back onto the atom's character offsets. Cues already stored still paint, since they are marked onto the segments before a cell ever reads them. Same 180-atom display cap as scripture; over the cap the citation line still navigates.
- **`block` on a verse-atom Book** follows the same shape: verse numbers **always** superscripted (as scripture's block does), the passage running as **book prose** — atoms are not set one verse per line, though an atom that carries `lines` keeps its own breaks inside that run (§11) — and the full-citation attribution line beneath. Unlike `inline` it **shows Line letters**, printed in the number slot the way the reader's gutter prints them ([ADR 0010](adr/0010-line-letters-are-locators-reading-order-may-interleave-atoms.md)), so a block quote can be followed against the print.
- **A multi-atom note walks the section's reading order** — the printed page, in which atoms may interleave ([ADR 0010](adr/0010-line-letters-are-locators-reading-order-may-interleave-atoms.md), encoded per [ADR 0013](adr/0013-section-reading-is-an-optional-page-order-list.md)) — never atom-id order. The verse superscript prints **at every step where the walk enters a different atom**, `inline` and `block` alike; a contiguous run of one atom prints one number, exactly as today. `{1 Enoch 5:6-7}` block prints ⁶6a 6b 6c ⁷7c ⁶6d …; `inline` prints the same walk with the letters omitted, ⁶ … ⁷ … ⁶ …, so a line whose letter is hidden is still locatable by its number.
- **Footnotes never render in a note** ([ADR 0012](adr/0012-book-footnotes-surface-in-the-study-panel.md)): chip, `inline` and `block` show neither a Footnote marker nor a Footnote body, whether the Book is dense with them (*1 Enoch*) or sparse (*Humility*'s three). Quoting an atom quotes the reading; the notes themselves surface only in the Study Panel for the selected atom.
- **The 180-atom display cap is unchanged** and applies to every Book alike, counted in atoms whatever the atom kind: over the cap, chip plus the "Reference too long to display" one-liner and no passage fetch, matching scripture's 180-verse cap and the Study Panel (§6). Reading order and Line letters do not enter the count — a step of the walk is not an atom.

## 5. Book reader

Visual reference: [book-reader-prototype](../prototypes/book-reader-prototype) (variant B typography; [NOTES.md](../prototypes/book-reader-prototype/NOTES.md) holds the verdict).

- **Typography**: serif book typography — centered chapter number + small-caps title, centered epigraph, justified prose, margin-gutter paragraph numbers.
- **Nav** reuses the scripture reader's existing tree/breadcrumb toggle (global default + in-pane switch). Tree side = the Book's **own section TOC** — the named sections of a paragraph Book (Preface / I–XII / Notes / Prayer), the chapter number alone where a verse-atom section carries no name (§1) — not a Books group inside the scripture tree. A book printed in Parts lists every section flat under **non-selectable Part group labels** (tree headings, `<optgroup>` in the breadcrumb), read off the section table's `part`; a book without Parts is one unlabelled run, exactly as before. **Headings** (Part titles, sub-section heads) render above the paragraph they are attached to, styled by level, outside the paragraph's own clickable body. An atom that keeps its own line breaks — a list's items, a flattened table's rows — prints them, whatever the layout: the breaks ride in the same `lines` channel scripture's poetry uses, and a Book's atom is line-structured by virtue of carrying one (the layout toggle governs scripture only). A **table** atom prints as the grid it was printed as: each of its rows carries its cells as spans over the atom's own text (`cells`, a blank cell a span of no width so the row keeps its columns), and the row the curator marked with a Markdown header rule prints as the column headings (`header`) — a table the printed work heads none of simply has no such row. The stored text is untouched by any of it, so Ref Spans, highlight offsets and the Search Index read a table exactly as they did when it printed as one flat run: a citation inside a cell still renders as its link, and a hit's matched words are still emphasized where they fall. A Heading is indexed with its paragraph's text (Search Index format version 3, which rebuilds every installed index once): a query whose words appear only there hits the paragraph, no phrase runs between a heading and the text, and the matched words are emphasized in the heading itself — in the result row and in the reader once the hit is activated. Breadcrumb side = `Humility › [section dropdown]` with prev/next steppers.
- **Reaching a book** (ticket #78): the trees and the breadcrumb stay as above — the crossing point is the ribbon icon's options panel, which lists every installed book in a **Books** section (absent when none is installed). Activating an entry opens the book in the reader tab already open, at the last position that reader held inside it or else its first section; Cmd/Ctrl-activating opens it in a reader tab of its own. The same modifier opens any reader tree node in its own tab.
- **Options**: Nav + font size + the Book's **atom-numbers option**, whose label and factory value follow the Book's atom kind (§1), so the gutter is never called "para" on a Book that prints verses (ticket 124). On a paragraph Book it is **Para numbers**, factory **Hover** — *Humility* and *IN*, unchanged. On a verse-atom Book it is **Verse numbers**, factory **On** — *1 Enoch* reads scripture-like while *Humility* stays quiet. Values are **On/Hover only**: no Off, no third visibility, and scripture's verse numbers stay always-on with no toggle of their own. The editorial-marks prototype hid verse numbers by default; that throwaway stub default is **explicitly not the factory** ([prototypes/editorial-marks-reader](../prototypes/editorial-marks-reader), ticket 116 — overridden for production by ticket 124), so an implementer does not copy it. Layout, Red letter, and Strong's are hidden for books. Single non-switchable edition pill (`Humility 1895`, `1 Enoch 1912`) replaces the translation switcher.
  - **Stored per installed Book × device**: one global value per Book per device (desktop / mobile, exactly like the per-device reader defaults below), edited on that Book's row in the settings **Books** section (§7) and **never written from the pane**. Flipping *1 Enoch* touches neither *Humility* nor any other verse-atom Book.
  - **Migration**: today's `readerParaNumbersDefault` — a single per-device value standing for every Book — **seeds every installed paragraph Book once on upgrade and is then removed**. A Book installed later gets the factory value for its atom kind, and a verse-atom Book never reads the old field, so a choice the reader already made survives and *1 Enoch* never inherits a paragraph Book's value.
  - **Pane**: the first visit to a Book in a pane seeds from that Book's per-device value; an in-pane flip is **ephemeral and scoped to that Book**, so pane state leaks to no other Book, no other pane, and never back into the stored default.
  - **Notes are unaffected**: `inline` and `block` print verse superscripts under §4's rules (multi-atom only inline, always in block), and the reader's On/Hover governs the reader gutter alone.
- **Per-device option defaults (scripture reader included)**: reader option global defaults split into desktop and mobile values; in-pane switching unchanged. Motivation: tree on desktop, breadcrumb on mobile.
- **Study Panel**, atom details: chip locator + full citation (the atom's own text stays in the reader, unrepeated); no Selection tab (a book has exactly one layer). Same chapter-scoped sections as scripture (§6), plus the selected atom's **Footnotes** when it carries any (§6, [ADR 0012](adr/0012-book-footnotes-surface-in-the-study-panel.md)).
- **Copy formatted reference**: a reader action (scripture *and* books) copying a paste-ready `{...}` reference for the selected verse/paragraph range — especially valuable for sections without printed numbers.

## 6. Cross-references, vault index & Study Panel

- **Runtime versification registry**: atom-counts-per-section becomes first-class runtime data. The compiled 66-book table is the permanently-registered base; each installed book module registers its manifest's atom-count table at load. All versification helpers (`verseCount`, `chapterCount`, `nextVerse`, `enumerateVerseIds`, `mergeRanges`, parse validation) consult the registry — intersection, indexing, and cross-references use the **same integer machinery** as scripture.
- **Dormant, not deleted**: a book ref parses only while its module is installed. Uninstall → occurrences drop from the vault index and chips show the unknown-book error; note text and cross-reference file entries are never touched; everything reappears on reinstall. Module install/uninstall triggers the existing debounced full reindex. No tombstones, no uninstall warnings.
- **Mixed cross-references are fully symmetric** — any member mix (scripture↔book, book↔book). Collect strip works identically in the book reader. Member labels use §4's display format; no member-kind badges or grouping. Clicking a book member opens the book reader at that atom. Members of an uninstalled book render a degraded label (e.g. `Book 101 5:2`) — `formatReference` never throws.
- **No Cross-References are ever seeded.** A Book module ships text, not edges: installing *1 Enoch* writes nothing into the vault's Cross-Reference file, and there is no Seed Pack. A student who wants `{Jude 14-15}` joined to `{1 Enoch 1:9}` makes that edge themselves with the mixed machinery above, and this spec takes no position on that pair (ticket 121).
- **Panel passages**: a book ref resolves its translation slot to the module's edition code (never `defaultTranslationId`, never switchable). `FallbackPassageSource` never substitutes for book ≥ 101 — a miss renders the explicit module-absent error. A reference of more than 180 atoms shows "Reference too long to display" instead of fetching the passage (same cap as note inline/block).
- **Section = chapter** for every chapter-scoped panel query (annotations, mentions, cross-references; Preface = 0). The book reader gets the same ● / ◆ trailing markers, per atom, alongside the gutter numbers.
- **Annotations need no new mechanism** — they work by construction once the grammar accepts book references.
- **Footnotes are a channel on the atom, not text of it** ([ADR 0012](adr/0012-book-footnotes-surface-in-the-study-panel.md)): `footnotes: [{ start, text }]` — the anchor offset into the atom's stored string plus the note body, and **no id**, because nothing addresses a note. A Footnote is **never a Reference** (it is not on the citable grid, §3) and **never a Hit**: the Search Index reads atom text plus Heading only (§5), so a word that appears only in Charles's note earns no hit, no result row and no emphasis. Notes are lifted out of the stored string at build (§2), so the reading a reader highlights is the reading the edition prints.
- **Placement is the Study Panel, and placement is the whole feature** ([ADR 0012](adr/0012-book-footnotes-surface-in-the-study-panel.md); [prototypes/book-footnotes-reader](../prototypes/book-footnotes-reader) variant A — [NOTES.md](../prototypes/book-footnotes-reader/NOTES.md) holds the verdict). **No marker prints on the page**: nothing at `start`, no superscript, no gutter glyph. Selecting an atom lists **exactly its attached notes** in the Study Panel (§5) under that atom's chip locator and full citation; an atom with none — *1 Enoch* 1:6 — shows no list at all, and an atom with several long ones — 1:9 — lists them all. The rule is one rule for a Book dense with notes (*1 Enoch*) and one sparse (*Humility*'s three), and there is **no On/Hover/Off**: placement is the default, not a setting. Notes in the vault suppress marker and body alike (§4). Rejected with the prototype: under-verse apparatus (variant B), a popover on the lemma (variant C), and any mix of C's marker onto A.
- **Highlights and Ref Spans bind the atom only.** With the notes lifted out, a highlight's offsets, a Ref Span (§8) and a hit's emphasis can never run into a Footnote. Footnote `start` and every `supplied` / `marks` / `emended` / `refs` offset index the **final** stored string — after *both* the Footnote lift and the Editorial-mark wrapper strip (§10). Which of the two lifts the build runs first is an implementation detail; the offsets are not.
- **How much of this is prior art, stated honestly**: only the **source syntax and the artifact shape**. `scripts/humility-pipeline` already lifts `[Footnote…]` / `[FootnoteN:]` out of the anchor paragraph onto `{ start, text }`, and the shipped `hum-m1895` artifact already carries *Humility*'s three notes — which no reader has ever been shown. But `StructuredVerse` has no `footnotes` field, nothing in `src/` reads one, and the shared `scripts/book-pipeline` (§2) has no lift at all. So the plugin-side channel — loader, Study Panel list, chip / `inline` / `block` suppression, Search Index exclusion — and the lift's generalization into the shared pipeline are **both new work**; Humility's parser is the syntax precedent and nothing more. The source marker stays that syntax, generalized: **not a new HTML wrapper** (the `<supplied>` / `<marks>` / `<emended>` family of §10 gains no `<footnote>`) and not a cite.

## 7. Module packaging, catalogue & settings

- **Manifest**: the existing `ModuleManifest` extended with `kind: 'book'` plus a required-when-book `book` sub-object — `{ number, editionCode, author, year, abbreviation, aliases?, atom?, sections: [{chapter, name, paragraphs, named?, part?, reading?}] }` (`part` §5, `reading` §11). `atom` is the Book's atom kind (`verse` | `paragraph`, absent = `paragraph`), copied from the Book Registry entry; `paragraphs` stays the atom-count field name whatever the kind, and neither is a format-version gain ([ADR 0009](adr/0009-optional-book-channels-do-not-bump-format-version.md)). One type, one store, one discovery path; `isTranslationManifest()` keeps translation-only code paths books-free. A book published or re-released from now on carries `BOOK_MODULE_FORMAT_VERSION`, which runs ahead of the shared `MODULE_FORMAT_VERSION` on the same field so a format gain of books alone (v6: Headings, and the Part each section sits under; v7: Figures, and the line channel a table or list keeps its rows on; v8: the cells a table's rows carry, and which row heads it) leaves installed translations untouched; `hum-m1895` was published before that gain and stays at 5, loading as a book whose sections simply have no Headings; capability flags all false/absent; `source`/`sourceChecksum` filled at download exactly like BSB. **The constant is spent only when an older installed Book would load *wrong*** ([ADR 0009](adr/0009-optional-book-channels-do-not-bump-format-version.md)): an optional atom channel an older Book loads correctly without — the Editorial-mark fields `supplied` / `marks` / `emended` (§10) — leaves it at **8**, as does the `footnotes` channel (§6), which `hum-m1895` already carries at **5**; and a once-off re-stamp of lagging Books at the current number is allowed if that shrinks implementation (content unchanged, constant still not bumped), never required.
- **Module id & storage**: id = edition code lowercased (`hum-m1895`) — a future re-cut edition coexists instead of overwriting. Storage layout unchanged: `modules/hum-m1895/manifest.json` + `101.json` keyed by atom verse-ids (`modules/1en-c1912/` + `103.json` for *1 Enoch*). Same `saveModule()` wipe-then-write flow.
- **Distribution** mirrors BSB per book: one release tag per book module (`hum-m1895-module`) carrying `hum-m1895-module.json` + `checksums.json`, sha256-verified. `BsbReleaseClient` generalizes into a parameterized release client (tag + filename + id) used by BSB and books; books register in the prebuilt-sources map so checksum-based update detection and cross-device re-download (`installedModuleIds` sync) work unchanged.
- **Catalogue**: compiled-in book catalogue array (v1: one entry; *1 Enoch*'s entry is the later implementation effort's, §2). A future remote catalogue replaces only the array.
- **Book Registry**: `scripts/book-registry.json` — append-only `{ bookNumber, title, author, moduleId, editionCode, atom? }` (`atom` optional, default `paragraph`); every book pipeline validates its manifest against it and fails the build on mismatch or number reuse. The *1 Enoch* entry is `{ bookNumber: 103, title: "1 Enoch", author: "Enoch", moduleId: "1en-c1912", editionCode: "1EN-C1912", atom: "verse" }`; its manifest `book` sub-object carries the same identity plus `year: 1912`, `abbreviation: "1En"` and `aliases: ["First Enoch", "Book of Enoch", "Ethiopic Enoch"]` (§1, §3). The text is US public domain and ships as a release artifact exactly like *Humility*'s (§2). The section/paragraph table is eyeballed before a book's first release (that release freezes the grid).
- **Settings**: a new **Books** section beside Translations — own row list (one row per catalogued Book; v1 shipped with Humility alone), same Download/Update/Delete row UX with shared busy/error states; no language filter, no Strong's badge. Rows show *title — author* with the edition code in the description, and each row carries that Book's **atom-numbers** default for this device — labelled **Verse numbers** or **Para numbers** after the Book's atom kind, On/Hover (§5) — which is the only place the value is written. Translation lists and both translation pickers stay books-free via the `kind` filter. The BSB first-run nudge stays translations-only.

## 8. Live scripture references in book content

- **Representation**: build-time-parsed **Ref Spans** — `{start, end, ranges}`: a character span over the atom's text plus pre-normalized verse ranges — stored in a `refs` channel beside the prose, the same pattern as the Strong's `tags`/`red`/`supplied` channels. Stored text stays clean; the reader does zero parsing. Epigraph refs take the same shape over the epigraph's text. No curly-brace grammar in module storage.
- **Linkify policy**: explicit citations only — the parser handles the uniform parenthetical patterns (`(John v. 19)`: roman chapter, arabic verse; lenient on the known missing-period case). A build-time **overrides file** (`scripts/humility-pipeline/ref-overrides.json`) supports *fix* (supply ranges for a mangled/missed ref) and *suppress* (false positive), keyed by `chapter.paragraph` — or `chapter.eN` for a section's Nth epigraph — plus the matched text; an override that no longer matches its atom exactly once fails the build, as does a bracketed citation the scanner resolved nothing in. Unreferenced allusions are never hand-annotated — Murray didn't cite them, we don't.
- **Reader behavior**: a quiet accent-colored link (the author's original citation text — no pill/chip in prose). Tap opens the scripture reader at the passage with the existing current-passage highlight + entry-context banner; Cmd/Ctrl-activating opens the target in a reader tab of its own, leaving the book pane where it stands. A hover/long-press preview popover is a possible later additive, not a different tap action.
- **Not Occurrences**: ref spans are reader-only live links; module content never enters the vault index.
- **No wording-mismatch mechanism**: the link is a citation, not a quotation-identity claim; navigation honors the user's translation like every other entry point.
- **Internal "(See Note A.)" pointers** linkify via the same machinery (ranges into book 101); navigation stays within the book reader.

## 9. Figures

- A **Figure** is a picture printed inside a section (CONTEXT.md — Figure): section furniture beside the grid, exactly like a Heading. It consumes no id, never joins an atom's text, is never searched, and is never citable — a reference addresses atoms only.
- **Attachment**: a figure rides on the paragraph it stands with and says which side it prints on — `place: 'above' | 'below'`. The curator writes it as a standalone Markdown image block, `![alt](in-images/tree-of-life.png "Fig 2 Tree of Life")`: it attaches to the paragraph that follows it, above it, the same rule a Heading follows; one that closes a section attaches below the last paragraph instead. The optional Markdown title is the printed caption. A caption the printed work sets as its own paragraph is left as the paragraph it already is — the figure simply stands above it.
- **Storage**: the image travels inside the module as a base64 data URI on the paragraph (`figures: [{ image, alt, caption?, place }]`), so an installed Book carries its own pictures and reads no file of its own. The image files themselves are committed beside the curated source (`resources/in-images/`) and read relative to it at build time; a figure whose image the build was not given fails the build. Book format only — `BOOK_MODULE_FORMAT_VERSION` v7, which leaves installed translations untouched.
- **Rendering**: the book reader prints a figure above or below its paragraph, outside the paragraph's own clickable body, with its caption beneath. Notes render text only — a citation quotes paragraphs, never plates.

## 10. Editorial marks

([ADR 0006](adr/0006-editorial-marks-in-the-atom.md), [ADR 0008](adr/0008-book-editorial-marks-are-html-wrappers-in-source.md), [ADR 0009](adr/0009-optional-book-channels-do-not-bump-format-version.md); Charles's key in [charles-1912-apparatus-and-source.md](research/charles-1912-apparatus-and-source.md); visual reference [prototypes/editorial-marks-reader](../prototypes/editorial-marks-reader), variant A — [NOTES.md](../prototypes/editorial-marks-reader/NOTES.md) holds the verdict.)

- **Editorial marks are the printed critical marks of a Book edition** (CONTEXT.md — Editorial marks), and they sit on the atom's characters: not a second layer, not Heading / Figure furniture. A critical Book stores **one string** per atom — the reading as the edition prints it, minus supplied delimiters — and nothing else: no diplomatic twin, no hide toggle of any kind, one layer under one Edition Code exactly as every other Book. A Book that prints none is **unmarked** — *Humility* and *IN* — and stays exactly as it is. *1 Enoch* is the first marked Book: Charles's key gives version brackets ⌈ ⌉ (in Gᵍ, not E) and ⌈⌈ ⌉⌉ (in E, not Gᵍ / Gˢ), restorations 〈 〉, interpolations [ ], editorial parentheses ( ), thick type for emendations, † † for corruption and … for lost words.
- **Three channels, baked at build; the reader parses nothing.** Every span indexes the *final* stored string — after the Footnote lift and the wrapper strip (§2, §6) — as `FormatSpan[]` beside the text, the way `refs` and Strong's `tags` already ride there (§8). An atom and an epigraph carry the same three fields; an unmarked Book omits all of them and grows no chrome for them.
  - **Supplied**: the delimiters an edition prints around words the editor added — Charles `( )`, BSB `[ ]` — are **stripped at build**; the words stay in the stored string and are identified in the translation `supplied` field, the very `FormatSpan[]` a Tagged Translation's supplied words use. No Book-only twin. Charles's `(even)` in 1:4 stores as `even`, supplied.
  - **Marks**: every other glyph **stays in the string** — ⌈ ⌉ ⌈⌈ ⌉⌉ 〈 〉 [ ] † … — and each is identified by one span in `marks?: FormatSpan[]`. The kind lives in the glyph itself: one channel for a version bracket and a dagger alike, no per-kind typing, no `interpolation` element.
  - **Emended**: the print's emphasis is not a character in the stored string; the emended words are identified in `emended?: FormatSpan[]`, exactly as supplied words are. Charles's `steadfast` in 2:2 stores as plain `steadfast`, emended.
- **Paint** is the quiet book page of §5 (prototype variant A) with three treatments, none of them a toggle:
  - **Supplied** words paint *italic* at **supplied-opacity**, in scripture and Books alike — Charles's `(even)` and BSB's `[Jesus]` share one treatment.
  - **Mark glyphs** paint in the **accent color** at **marks-opacity** — visible, never shouting.
  - **Emended** words paint **bold**: full weight, unfaded, un-accented — emendation is weight, not another faded glyph.
  - Accent glyphs, italic supplied and bold emendation are **readable together** on one verse (1:4: supplied *even* plus the interpolation `[And appear from His camp]`; 2:2: emended *steadfast* beside marks) without any hide; the density of ⌈ ⌉ in 1:9 is accepted at marks-opacity 0.3. Rejected with the prototype: a per-verse margin key of mark kinds (variant B), the scripture column (variant C), and any toggle that hides the marks.
  - **No tooltip on a mark glyph.** Prototype variant A's hover text naming Charles's key is not carried forward: a gloss needs a glyph→kind table, which the one-channel model above refuses, and hover never reaches a touch screen. Marks are paint only; the key belongs to a later key page outside the grid (§1).
- **Two new global settings**, each a single user-adjustable value, both **new** — no opacity setting exists today: the scripture reader's `.scripture-study-supplied` is italic only, at full opacity. ADR 0006 and the prototype NOTES call the supplied setting "existing"; that was wrong about the code, not about the decision.
  - **Supplied-opacity**, factory **0.5**, **shared by scripture and Books**: one value moves every supplied word everywhere. **Scripture-side upgrade effect, stated outright:** on upgrade, BSB's supplied words dim from full opacity to 0.5 for every existing user; a user who wants them back at full strength raises the setting.
  - **Marks-opacity**, factory **0.3**, **marks-only**: it fades mark glyphs and nothing else, so Charles's brackets can be dimmed without touching BSB's supplied words. Emended words listen to neither setting.
  - **Placement**: both live in the settings tab's **Reader defaults** group, after Paragraph numbers — *Supplied words opacity* and *Editorial marks opacity*. Supplied-opacity moves BSB scripture too, so a Books group would mislabel it, and Highlights is the wash's home, a different thing. Each is one slider, **5–100 % in steps of 1**, reusing the Highlight Wash's range constants and validation (0 % would hide, which the paint rule above forbids), written on release like the wash sliders. **No reset row**: two sliders need none; the Highlights reset stays the palette's.
- **Highlights, Hits and Ref Spans bind the stored string, marks included.** A mark glyph and a supplied word are characters of the atom, so a highlight's offsets count them, a Search Query finds `even`, a hit emphasizes matched words wherever they fall among the brackets, and a Ref Span may run over them. Emended words are ordinary text to all three. Hits present in Canonical Grid order as ever. What is *not* in the string never participates: Line letters (§11, [ADR 0010](adr/0010-line-letters-are-locators-reading-order-may-interleave-atoms.md), ticket 125) and Footnotes (§6, [ADR 0012](adr/0012-book-footnotes-surface-in-the-study-panel.md), ticket 127). Rejected: leaving supplied brackets in the string and hiding them at render — offsets would count characters the reader does not show.
- **Source** ([ADR 0008](adr/0008-book-editorial-marks-are-html-wrappers-in-source.md)): marks are never inferred from the printed glyphs. The curator wraps the three channels as HTML named elements in atom text and in epigraphs — `<supplied>`, `<marks>`, `<emended>` — and the shared pipeline (§2) emits the spans. Wrappers are never stored; the inner text is the span. Charles's 1:2 `⌈⌈which⌉⌉` is written `<marks>⌈⌈</marks>which<marks>⌉⌉</marks>`, 1:4's `(even)` is `<supplied>even</supplied>`, and 2:2's thick type inside its version bracket is `<marks>⌈</marks>how <emended>steadfast</emended> they are<marks>⌉</marks>`. In a verse-atom Book the wrappers sit after the `N.` / `Na.` line prefix and are parsed on the joined atom (§11, [ADR 0011](adr/0011-verse-atom-source-uses-n-na-prefixes.md), ticket 126).
  - **One `<marks>` per stay-glyph**: a bracket pair is two wrappers, one on each glyph, never one wrapper around the enclosed words — the enclosed words are the reading, not a mark.
  - **Well-formed per atom**: exact lowercase element names, no attributes; nesting is allowed (a supplied word inside an interpolation, an emended word inside a version bracket).
  - **A run that would cross atoms closes at the first atom's end and reopens at the next** — a Book never inherits BSB's cross-verse open state; each atom's spans stand alone.
  - **The build fails, citing the atom**, on: overlapping wrappers; an unknown element; an empty wrap (`<marks></marks>`); a raw `<` anywhere in atom text or an epigraph. **It fails, citing the furniture**, on a wrapper inside a Heading, a figure caption, or a `{named}` section head — furniture never carries Editorial marks. Rejected: `(even)` / `=word=` conventions, `{named}`-family braces, Charles-typed elements, HTML `<mark>`.
- **Format version** ([ADR 0009](adr/0009-optional-book-channels-do-not-bump-format-version.md)): `BOOK_MODULE_FORMAT_VERSION` **stays 8**. `supplied`, `marks` and `emended` are general optional fields on every Book atom and epigraph — not a 1 Enoch-only schema — and an older Book loads correctly without them. **No capability flags**: a Book manifest declares no `suppliedWords`, `marks` or `emended` flag; the presence of spans is the only signal. *Humility* stays at **5** and *IN* at **8**, unmarked; *1 Enoch* publishes at 8 and populates the fields. A later bump is spent only when an older installed Book would load *wrong*, never because it merely lacks a new optional channel. A once-off re-stamp of lagging Books at the current book format version is **allowed, not required** — content unchanged, constant still not bumped — and Editorial marks do not call for it (§7).

## 11. Poetic lineation, Line letters & section reading order

([ADR 0007](adr/0007-book-poetic-lineation-uses-scripture-lines-channel.md), [ADR 0010](adr/0010-line-letters-are-locators-reading-order-may-interleave-atoms.md), [ADR 0011](adr/0011-verse-atom-source-uses-n-na-prefixes.md), [ADR 0013](adr/0013-section-reading-is-an-optional-page-order-list.md), [ADR 0014](adr/0014-book-sections-stand-in-numeric-order.md); Charles's lineation in [charles-1912-volume-anatomy.md](research/charles-1912-volume-anatomy.md).)

- **A Book that prints poetry keeps its lineation on the atom** ([ADR 0007](adr/0007-book-poetic-lineation-uses-scripture-lines-channel.md)). One atom per printed verse (§1); the stored text is the verse **space-joined** into one string, and the metrical-line starts ride in the same `lines` channel scripture's poetry uses and a Book's lists and tables already use (§5) — **flush**, no BSB indent level. Stanza blanks are `paragraph: true` on the group's first line, so 1:8–9 and 5:9 keep Charles's stanza breaks without a new atom delimiter. A **prose lead-in in a poetic verse is line 0 of the same atom** — 1:3's prose opening and its tristichs are one atom and one cite. *1 Enoch* is the instance: Charles's Preface claims the poetic structure, and I. 3–9 prints "a poem consisting of tristichs". Rejected: flattening to one prose run; list-style `\n` in the stored string (Highlights would count layout); treating a page-wrap as a line.
- **An atom that carries `lines` always prints its breaks** — in the reader and in notes' `inline` / `block` alike — because a Book's atom is line-structured by virtue of carrying the channel (§5). The scripture reader's **Layout** toggle stays scripture-only and is hidden for Books (§5): book poetry is not a toggle.
- **Line letters are locators** (CONTEXT.md — Line letter; [ADR 0010](adr/0010-line-letters-are-locators-reading-order-may-interleave-atoms.md)): Charles labels metrical lines `6a`, `7c`. A letter is **not an atom** (`{1 Enoch 5:6a}` is not a cite, §3), **not an Editorial mark** (§10), and **not stored text** — it lives as `letter` on the line (below), never in the string, so Highlights, Hits and Ref Spans never count gutter chrome (§10).
  - **Gutter**: a lettered line paints `6a` / `7c` **in the verse-number slot, in place of the number**, at the same On/Hover the Book's verse numbers use (§5, tickets 124 and 125) — no second knob. An **unlettered line keeps ordinary verse chrome**: the number on the atom's first line, nothing on the rest. Rejected: letters in the stored string; dropping them.
  - **Missing letters are never invented**: where Charles printed `6a … 6g` and no `6h`, no `6h` exists in the module or on the page; a verse he printed without letters stays unlettered. The gutter names only what the print names.
- **Letter order versus page order** ([ADR 0010](adr/0010-line-letters-are-locators-reading-order-may-interleave-atoms.md)): an atom's stored `text` and `lines` are **letter order** — 5:7 is `7a`, `7b`, `7c` in that sequence, so `{1 Enoch 5:7}` as a single atom is still the traditional verse as one reading. A **section's reading order is Charles's page**, in which atoms may interleave: chapter 5 walks `6a 6b 6c 7c 6d …`, a line of verse 7 printed inside verse 6's poem. The chapter reader walks that page; so does a multi-atom note, with the verse superscript at every entry into a different atom (§4). **Opening `{1 Enoch 5:7}` lands on 7c** — the atom's first appearance on the page — not on a reconstructed `7a`–`7c` block; the whole atom is selected and cited as ever. **Hits still present in Canonical Grid order**, grouped by book (CONTEXT.md — Hit): page order is a reader and notes walk, never a search-order rewrite. Rejected: grouping the reader by atom; storing dislocated text on the printed neighbour; page order as the atom string (neither the traditional verse nor the page).
- **Cross-chapter dislocation is furniture, not a walk** ([ADR 0014](adr/0014-book-sections-stand-in-numeric-order.md)). `reading` walks one section and no cross-section walk exists. Charles prints the Epistle out of numeric sequence — 92, then 91:1–11 and 18–19, then 93, then 91:12–17, then 94 — but *1 Enoch*'s sections stand in **numeric order** (§1), 91 → 92 → 93, each chapter's verses in numeric order, and the Title Bar steps that order. The numbering is Dillmann's and shared by every edition (Knibb, Isaac, Nickelsburg/VanderKam), all of which print numeric order, so a reader arriving from any of them finds the chapters where they expect; the page rearrangement is Charles's own reconstruction, of which Qumran 4Q212 later confirmed only the 93 → 91:12–17 half. His section Headings survive **verbatim** as furniture (§1) — *XCII. XCI. 1–10, 18–19. Enoch's Book of Admonition for his Children* on 92:1, *XCI. 1–11, 18–19. Enoch's Admonition to his Children* on 91:1, *XCIII, XCI. 12–17. The Apocalypse of Weeks* on 93:1, *XCI. 12–17. The Last Three Weeks* on 91:12 — so the dislocation is visible without being enforced. Within-chapter interleaving (5:6–7 above) is unaffected. Rejected: section order following the page at section level (loses the split of 91); a Book-level `reading` walk (Title Bar stepping 93 → 91 → 94, a second reading concept for one editor's conjecture); renumbering.
- **Module encoding** ([ADR 0013](adr/0013-section-reading-is-an-optional-page-order-list.md)):
  - `letter?: string` on the line (`"a"`), omitted when unlettered — the letter is on the line, not in the stored string and not on the reading step.
  - `reading?: { atom, line? }[]` is **optional on `BookSection`** (manifest, §7), the section's full walk in page order. `atom` is the `VVV` within that section; `line` is the 0-based index into that atom's letter-order `lines`; `line` is **omitted only for a whole prose atom** — one that carries no `lines`. Absent `reading` means atoms `1..N`, each atom's `lines` in stored order — *Humility*, *IN* and most *1 Enoch* chapters. **Present only when that walk is not the page**; an identity permutation is never stored, so a normal chapter grows no no-op list. A present list is the whole section walk, never a partial patch.
  - **Build checks when `reading` is present**, failing and citing the section: every atom `1..N` appears at least once; an atom with `lines` has each index exactly once and no whole-atom step; an atom without `lines` has exactly one `{ atom }` step; every `line` is in range; a duplicate `(atom, line)` fails.
  - **Loader**: present → that walk; absent → `1..N` × stored `lines`, no code path a paragraph Book does not already take.
  - **No format-version bump** ([ADR 0009](adr/0009-optional-book-channels-do-not-bump-format-version.md)): `letter` and `reading` are optional fields an older Book loads correctly without, so `BOOK_MODULE_FORMAT_VERSION` stays **8** (§7, §10). Rejected: an `order` on the line; a sidecar `reading.json`; a pointer by `start` or by letter.
- **Source** ([ADR 0011](adr/0011-verse-atom-source-uses-n-na-prefixes.md)): a verse-atom Book's curated Markdown (§2) marks lines with `N.` / `Na.` prefixes in **Charles's page order**, so `7c.` written between `6c.` and `6d.` *is* the reading walk: the build stores each atom's lines in **letter order** whatever their position on the page ([ADR 0010](adr/0010-line-letters-are-locators-reading-order-may-interleave-atoms.md)) and emits `reading` only where the page order differs from that identity. The Book's atom kind (§1, §7) selects this parser — no new front matter.
  - In a verse-atom section a body line is either a **verse-line**, `^(\d+)([a-z])?\.\s+.+`, or a **wrap** — an unprefixed line in the same block, space-joined onto the previous verse-line, because a page-wrap is not a metrical line. The digit assigns the line to that atom in the current `##` section (the chapter is the `##` section head, never in the prefix); the letter is stripped as the line's `letter`. Prefixes never enter stored text.
  - The prefix is **repeated on every metrical line** — `6a.` / `6b.` / `6c.` for a lettered poem, `3.` / `3.` / `3.` for an unlettered one. A **prose verse is one prefix plus wraps** and carries no `lines`. A **mixed verse (1:3)** is an unlettered lead-in followed by `N.` / `Na.` lines — unlettered lines come only before that atom's lettered ones.
  - A **blank line sets `paragraph: true` on the next verse-line**, and a section's first verse-line carries it too; a blank line is never an atom delimiter. `1.` means **verse 1**, never a numbered list: the paragraph pipeline's line-keeping list rule does not run in a verse-atom section, and lists and tables fail the build there. Editorial-mark wrappers sit **after the prefix** and are parsed on the joined atom, so marks and lineation compose (§10).
  - **The build fails, citing the line**, on: a line with no prefix that is not a wrap; a malformed prefix (`6A.`, `6aa.`) or a prefix with empty text; a duplicate letter within an atom; an unlettered line after a lettered one in the same atom; a hole in `1..N` within the section; a list or table. **Allowed**: page-order jumps (`7c.` inside verse 6) and missing letters (`6a`, `6c` with no `6b`) — Charles's dislocations and incomplete lettering survive and are never "fixed". Rejected: an HTML `<line>` element; indent-as-poetry; `5:6a` in the prefix.
- **Paragraph Books are unchanged**: *Humility* and *IN* carry no `letter`, no `reading`, and keep their blank-line paragraph atoms, `1.` lists and tables exactly as they build today.

## 12. Implementation notes

Surfaced by the code scan while resolving cross-reference semantics:

- `formatReference`/`bookName` currently throw for book ≥ 67 and sit on hot paths (xref labels, passage cache keys) — must degrade instead (§6).
- `mergeRanges`/`enumerateVerseIds` silently degrade without registry data — the versification registry must be in place before book refs parse.
- Occurrence indexing is already book-agnostic once parsing succeeds.
- Per-device reader defaults (§5) touch the scripture reader's settings surface, not just books.

Surfaced by the code scan for the verse-atom and Editorial-marks work (§1, §5, §6, §10, §11):

- `src/rendering/module-passage-source.ts` is the one segmenter: it cuts each verse by its `red` and `supplied` spans and hands `segment.supplied` to three renderers (`src/reader/ReaderPane.svelte`, `src/study-material/VerseDetails.svelte`, `src/rendering/render-reference.ts`), and `styles.css`'s `.scripture-study-supplied` is italic only, at full opacity. `marks` and `emended` (§10) and both opacity settings must enter that segmenter and that stylesheet, not a Book-side paint, or highlights and table cells (§5) stop composing with them.
- `src/reader/reader-feature.ts` seeds every reader pane's `toggles.paraNumbers` from `settings.readerParaNumbersDefault[device]` — scripture panes included — and `src/data-access/migrate-reader-defaults.ts` is the existing settings-migration seam. That seed line is where a stale global would leak onto *1 Enoch*; the per-Book value (§5) replaces it for Books and the migration retires the field.
- `src/modules/verse-content.ts` `StructuredVerse` carries `text`, `tags`, `lines`, `red`, `supplied`, `refs`, `headings`, `figures` and nothing else: no `footnotes`, `marks`, `emended`, no `letter` on `VerseLine`, and `BookSection` no `reading`. It is the single type seam every new channel lands in (§6, §10, §11).
- `src/reference/book-citation.ts` prints `ch. N, par. V` for every Book and sits on the chip path of every note re-render; the atom kind (§1) must reach it from the manifest before a verse-atom chip can read *1 Enoch* 1:9 (§4).

## Out of scope

- **Book content as a Mentions source** (indexing module-internal scripture refs as Occurrences so the scripture reader surfaces *Humility* citations) — a future effort of its own; the stored representation doesn't foreclose it.
- **A second English *1 Enoch*** (ticket 120) — deliberately unspecified. One Book is one module is one grid, so another English of the same work is not a Translation of `1EN-C1912`, gets no reader switcher, and is not designed here as a second Book's UX either. This spec is likewise silent on registry title uniqueness and on what `{1 Enoch}` would mean if two such Books were ever installed; reopen when one is actually wanted.
- **A general (remote) catalogue** — v-next; the packaging design only leaves room for it (parameterized release client, registry, `kind` filter). The second-book pipeline this bullet once paired with is no longer future: the shared pipeline is the Book path (§2).
