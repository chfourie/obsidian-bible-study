# Charles 1912 *The Book of Enoch; or, 1 Enoch* — Volume Anatomy

Research for the *1 Enoch* book module (ADR 0005 freezes R.H. Charles 1912 numbering as the citable grid). Date: 2026-09-08. Claims cite the 1912 Clarendon volume (or a faithful scan of it); the 1917 SPCK popular reprint is used only where it preserves the same translation / to diagnose Wikisource.

## Summary & Recommendation

- **Main text** = Charles’s English **translation of printed chapters I–CVIII** (verses numbered; poetic passages lineated). That includes the Noah fragment (CVI–CVII) and ch. CVIII titled as an appendix *to the Book of Enoch* — still in the 1–108 chapter sequence. Only this layer is a candidate for the citable grid (`{1 Enoch …}`).
- **Not main text** (Charles’s apparatus / scholarship): Dedication; Charles’s **Preface**; **General Introduction** (pp. ix–cx); **Abbreviations, Brackets, and Symbols**; per-section **Special Introductions**; per-verse **critical and exegetical footnotes**; **Appendix I** (Gizeh / Syncellus Greek); **Appendix II** (Son of Man essay); **Indexes I–II**.
- Spec may still ship intro / footnotes / appendix **as module content**; they are **not** referencable atoms.
- **Wikisource “Chapter 109”** is a **TOC error**, not a real chapter. Sacred-texts (same 1917 transcription lineage) correctly ends at CVIII under “An Appendix to the Book of Enoch.” Charles 1912 has no CIX.
- **Build authority**: Internet Archive scan `bookofenochor1en00char` (Clarendon Press, 1912; Robarts / University of Toronto) — full scholarly volume. Do **not** treat Wikisource / sacred-texts as the 1912 book-as-object (they follow the **1917 SPCK** slim reprint of the translation).

## Anatomy of the 1912 Volume

### Front matter (Charles, not Enoch)

1. **Title page / imprint** — Clarendon Press, 1912.
2. **Dedication** — “To the Warden and Fellows of Merton College.”
3. **Preface** — Charles; explains 1906 Ethiopic text base, poetical structure as a “new and revolutionary feature,” dated June 1, 1912. *Not* labelled “Editors’ Preface” in 1912.
4. **Contents** — maps General Introduction ix–cx; “The Book of Enoch — Special Introductions, Translation, Critical and Exegetical Notes” **1–272**; then appendices/indexes (see below).
5. **General Introduction** (pp. **ix–cx**) — §§1–20 (Short Account; Title; Canonicity; Greek Versions; Ethiopic MSS; Critical Inquiries; Different Elements; Poetical Element; Original Language; Influence; Theology; …).
6. **Abbreviations, Brackets, and Symbols specially used in the Translation of 1 Enoch** — sigla (`Gᵍ` Gizeh/Akhmím Greek, `Gˢ` Syncellus, `E` Ethiopic, MSS classes α/β) and bracket conventions (`⌈⌉`, `††`, `⟨⟩`, `[]`, `()`, thick type for emendations). Immediately precedes Section I.

### Body: five sections + Noah fragment + ch. 108 (pp. 1–272)

Charles prints **five constituent books/sections**, each with a **Special Introduction** then **Translation and Critical and Exegetical Notes**. Chapter division into **108** is Dillmann’s (Charles: “without MSS. authority… adopted for the sake of convenience,” Intro. §7).

| Section | Chapters | Contents page / start | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | i–xxxvi | p. **1** (intro); translation from p. **4** | Book of the Watchers (+ journeys XVII–XXXVI) |
| II | xxxvii–lxxi | p. **64** / **69** | The Parables (incl. internal Noah material Charles isolates critically) |
| III | lxxii–lxxxii | p. **147** / **151** | Courses of the Heavenly Luminaries |
| IV | lxxxiii–xc | p. **179** / **182** | Dream-Visions |
| V | xci–civ (+ cv) | p. **218** / **224** | Concluding section; **105** treated as “independent fragment” in Intro. §12 |

**After the five books, still inside pp. 1–272:**

- **CVI–CVII** — printed under the running Section V pagination; Intro. §12: “106–107… part of the Book of Noah”; translation heading sequence reaches **Chapter CVI / CVII** (OCR ~pp. 264–269). Sacred-texts / 1917 TOC label: **“Fragment of the Book of Noah.”**
- **CVIII** — Intro. §12: “**108. This chapter forms an Appendix to the entire work**, added not by the editor but by a subsequent writer….” Translation opens **CHAPTER CVIII.** / “Another book which Enoch wrote…” (~pp. 269–272). Sacred-texts TOC: **“An Appendix to the Book of Enoch” → Chapter CVIII.**

**Verse numbers** are printed throughout (e.g. `I. 1.`, `I. 2.` …). **Poetic lineation**: Preface claims discovery of poetical structure; at **I. 3–9** the notes say “A poem consisting of tristichs”; English lines are broken as verse (also clear in the 1917 sacred-texts page for ch. I, which reprints that translation).

**Footnotes**: critical/exegetical notes sit with the translation (variant readings, emendations, commentary) — apparatus, not body verses.

## Wikisource “Chapter 109”

**Verdict: TOC error — no real chapter.**

Evidence:

1. Charles 1912 adopts a **108**-chapter scheme and never prints CIX; translation block ends at CVIII before App. I at p. 273.
2. Intro. §12 explicitly calls **108** the appendix to the entire work.
3. Sacred-texts (Wikisource’s stated source) TOC:

   - Fragment of the Book of Noah → **CVI, CVII**
   - An Appendix to the Book of Enoch → **CVIII**
4. Wikisource TOC mistakenly groups **108** under “Fragment of the Book of Noah” and adds a redlinked **[/Chapter 109](https://en.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=The_Book_of_Enoch_(Charles)/Chapter_109&action=edit&redlink=1)** under “An Appendix…”. The live **[/Chapter_108](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Book_of_Enoch_(Charles)/Chapter_108)** page *is* the appendix text (“Another book which Enoch wrote…”).

So “109” is a mis-placed heading, not an extra chapter in 1912 or 1917.

### Main-text definition (for the module)

**Main text** = the English translation verses of **chapters 1–108** as printed in Charles 1912 (same numbering in the 1917 reprint of that translation): arabic verse numbers, poetic line breaks where Charles sets poetry, including Noah fragment and ch. 108. Section headings and Charles’s notes are scaffolding around that grid.
